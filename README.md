# Asymmetric Variance: A Six Sigma Approach to Football Analytics
> "Victory is achieved when we operate within Six Sigma reliability (Order), while forcing the opponent into High-Entropy states (Chaos)."
## 📄 Project Overview
This repository hosts the **Asymmetric Variance Framework**, a football analytics methodology that applies **Industrial Engineering, Reliability Engineering (Six Sigma), and Game Theory** to modern tactics. 
The objective is not just to analyze data, but to inject a precise engineering mindset into the sport—moving beyond the fatalistic acceptance of variance.

## Project 2: Real Madrid vs Barcelona — UCL 2012–2018, A Six Sigma Comparative Study
> 1 Research background: the Six Sigma framing for elite team comparison and the central question — why did RM win 4 UEFA Champions League titles while BA won only one during the same era, despite both teams being considered the best in the world?
> 2, Data sources, scope, and key limitations relative to Project 1's Statsbomb foundation
> 3, Five-pillar analytical framework: each team's attacking chain is evaluated through Passing Analysis, Danger Pass (connecting pass quality to shot creation), and Shot Analysis; Defending Analysis captures how each team imposed Entropy on their opponents; Valued Action synthesizes the style fingerprint to explain the underlying mechanisms behind RM's Order and BA's higher Entropy across six UEFA Champions League seasons

> 9, Tactical and structural synthesis: how RM's system generated Six Sigma reliability (Order) while BA's style produced higher Entropy — the tactical and structural reasons behind the quality control gap that decided four championships

> 11, Future plan: introducing an AI agent to automate the comparative study framework for 2019–2024 UEFA Champions League seasons — examining how both teams' quality control evolved and why RM's relative Order was sufficient to claim 2 titles despite declining standards across both clubs; Parts 1 and 2 serve as the methodological foundation for this pipeline, enabling readers and researchers to replicate and extend the analysis significantly faster than the manual process
